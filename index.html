The Material Risk of
Solid-State Batteries

Solid-state batteries with solid-state electrolytes promise safer, denser energy storage. But every candidate electrolyte chemistry depends on a different supply chain. This is a story about where those materials come from, who controls them, and what that means for the next generation of electric vehicles.

Scroll ↓
01

Battery demand is increasing.

Global battery demand has more than quintupled since 2020, driven almost entirely by the adoption electric vehicles. China accounts for the majority of that growth.

02

But which battery?

A "solid-state electrolyte" isn't one technology, it's at least three competing chemistries, each built on a different electrolyte material. The choice isn't just technical. It rewrites the entire supply chain.

OXIDE-BASED (e.g. LLZO)

Lithium · Lanthanum · Zirconium · Oxide

High stability and wide voltage window. But lanthanum is a rare earth, and rare earth production is the most geographically concentrated supply chain of the five.

  • 🔋Lithium — also in smartphone batteries, psychiatric medication (lithium carbonate), and ceramics
  • 🔭Lanthanum — also in camera and telescope lenses, hydrogen storage alloys, and EV motor magnets
  • ✈️Zirconium — also in nuclear reactor cladding, jet engine coatings, and ceramic knives
SULFIDE-BASED (e.g. Li₆PS₅Cl)

Lithium · Sulfur · Phosphate

Highest ionic conductivity. Sulfur and phosphate are the most geographically distributed inputs, drawn from dozens of producing countries.

  • 🔋Lithium — also in smartphone batteries, psychiatric medication, and ceramics
  • 🌋Sulfur — also in fertilizers, rubber vulcanization, gunpowder, and pharmaceutical production
  • 🌾Phosphate — also in crop fertilizers (feeds ~half the world's population), detergents, and food preservatives
PHOSPHATE-BASED (LAGP)

Lithium · Aluminum · Germanium · Phosphate

A middle ground. Phosphate is well-distributed, but germanium introduce significant concentration risk.

  • 🔋Lithium — also in smartphone batteries, psychiatric medication, and ceramics
  • ✈️Aluminum — also in aircraft fuselages, beverage cans, window frames, and foil wrap
  • 💻Germanium — also in fiber optic cables, infrared night-vision equipment, and solar panels
  • 🌾Phosphate — also in crop fertilizers, detergents, and food preservatives

04

What is the concentration risk?

The Herfindahl–Hirschman Index (HHI) is the standard measure of market concentration. Higher means fewer countries control more of the supply. A score above 2,500 is considered "highly concentrated." Rare earths clear that bar by nearly double.

05

A decade of price changes.

Over the last decade, rare earth prices collapsed by more than 80% as Chinese production scaled up. Sulfur swung wildly. Lithium doubled. Each of these moves can reshape which battery chemistry is commercially viable for the timeframe.

07

What this means.

There is no "clean" solid-state chemistry, supply-chain-wise. Every path forward trades one kind of risk for another.

Sulfide electrolytes are the safest bet on geographic diversity, sulfur and phosphate are produced almost everywhere. But they come with processing challenges this visualization doesn't capture.

Oxide electrolytes buy chemical stability at the cost of deep dependence on Chinese rare earth production, a dependence that has already triggered export controls.

As EV demand climbs, material choices made in battery labs today will determine which countries hold pricing power for decades. This dashboard is a starting point for asking better questions about those choices.
